      Remarks: Carbohydrates are explicitly identified as the chief energy providers in human food. They supply the primary fuel needed for bodily functions compared to other nutrients which serve different biological roles.

Q3. [EASY] Which of the following substances is synthesized by the liver?

  ✓ A. Heparin
      Remarks: The liver synthesizes various plasma proteins and specific specialized compounds like the anticoagulant heparin. This distinguishes its synthetic output from purely exocrine secretions produced elsewhere in the digestive system.
    B. Trypsinogen
    C. Insulin
    D. Glucagon

Q4. [MEDIUM] What is the primary function of hydrochloric acid in the stomach regarding enzymatic activity?

  ✓ A. It provides the optimal acidic pH of 1.8 required for the activation and action of pepsin.
      Remarks: Hydrochloric acid provides the specific acidic pH of 1.8 which is optimal for the action of pepsin. Furthermore, exposure to hydrochloric acid converts the inactive proenzymes pepsinogen and prorennin into their active enzyme forms, pepsin and rennin.
    B. It directly converts dietary proteins into smaller peptones without the assistance of any other enzymes.
    C. It neutralizes the acidic contents of the stomach to protect the mucosal epithelium from excoriation.
    D. It breaks down complex starches into maltose molecules through enzymatic hydrolysis.

Q5. [MEDIUM] Which nutritional disorder is caused by a dietary deficiency of proteins alone, characterized by fluid accumulation in intercellular tissue spaces?

  ✓ A. Kwashiorkor
      Remarks: Kwashiorkor is specifically caused by a deficiency of proteins in the diet. This protein shortage leads to the accumulation of fluid in the intercellular spaces of body tissues, resulting in symptoms such as oedema and swelling.
    B. Marasmus
    C. Indigestion
    D. Jaundice

Q6. [EASY] Which term describes the complete processes leading to and including the chemical breakdown of food materials to provide energy for life?

  ✓ A. Respiration
      Remarks: Respiration is defined more accurately as the comprehensive set of processes leading to, and including, the chemical breakdown of food materials to release energy for life. This encompasses both the delivery of gases and the subsequent internal breakdown reactions.
    B. Ventilation
    C. Homeostasis
    D. Circulation

Q7. [MEDIUM] Which structural modification is formed by the mucosa of the stomach?

  ✓ A. Gastric rugae
      Remarks: The mucosa of the stomach forms irregular folds known as gastric rugae, with numerous microscopic gastric glands situated in between them. This mucosal architecture is distinct from the villi found in the small intestine.
    B. Villi
    C. Crypts of Lieberkuhn
    D. Microvilli

Q8. [EASY] Which enzyme is responsible for converting inactive trypsinogen into active trypsin?

  ✓ A. Enterokinase
      Remarks: Trypsinogen is activated into active trypsin by the enzyme enterokinase. Enterokinase is specifically secreted by the intestinal mucosa to initiate this activation cascade.
    B. Chymotrypsinogen
    C. Carboxypeptidase
    D. Steapsin

Q9. [EASY] Which anatomical structure guards the opening between the stomach and the small intestine?

  ✓ A. The pyloric sphincter
      Remarks: The pyloric sphincter guards the pyloric aperture, which is the opening through which the posterior pyloric portion of the stomach connects to the first part of the small intestine. This structure regulates the passage of contents leaving the stomach.
    B. The ilio-caecal valve
    C. The internal anal sphincter
    D. The external anal sphincter

  ✓ A. Increased thoracic volume leads to increased pulmonary volume, decreasing intra-pulmonary pressure below atmospheric pressure.

    B. Decreased thoracic volume leads to decreased pulmonary volume, increasing intra-pulmonary pressure above atmospheric pressure.
    C. Relaxation of the diaphragm increases pulmonary volume, raising intra-pulmonary pressure above atmospheric levels.
    D. Contraction of internal intercostal muscles decreases pulmonary volume and forces outside air inward.
